## 7.10 The same evaluation at T = 400, and the efficiency-first τ

The §7.2–7.7 paired-event scans run at T = 30 (the 1BQF statevector cost: one T = 400 solve is 22 qubits, ≈ 360 s on GPU). This subsection repeats the evaluation at the production multiplicity **T = 400**, and adds the operating point the project doctrine actually wants — a **per-solver efficiency-first τ**, the largest absolute τ whose segment efficiency is still ≥ 99% (so efficiency stays high and purity stays as high as that target allows), with the false rate paid quoted. Scripts: `gen_T400_eval.py` → `figures/epsilon_sensitivity/T400_*.png` + `outputs/T400_eval.json`, on commit `cf843162`.

<callout icon="⚙️" color="gray_bg">
	**Why T = 400 is split into a fresh classical sweep + a store read.** The classical solve at T = 400 is cheap (spsolve, ≈ 0.1–0.8 s at the formula ε, n_seg = 640 000), so the **direct ε-sweep is run fresh**. But two things bite: (1) at ε ≳ 2× the formula value the matrix goes **dense** — A_nnz explodes and spsolve runs minutes — so the T = 400 ε-sweep uses a per-cell grid of 0.3–1.5× the formula ε (the formula ε itself keeps A_nnz ≈ n_seg, the sparse-invariant regime); (2) the **1BQF at T = 400 is a GPU campaign**, so the quantum side is read from the store's existing T = 400 statevector solves (formula ε) rather than re-solved over a swept ε. Store quantum coverage at T = 400: 1–3 reps for σ_res ≤ 0.02 (all σ_scatt); **σ_res = 0.05 quantum is absent** (the GPU-lane shortfall), so the worst-noise row is classical-only here.
</callout>

### The efficiency-first τ — both solvers, both operating points

![T=400 working points: segment efficiency and false rate vs sigma_res (fixed sigma_scatt) and vs sigma_scatt (fixed sigma_res), classical and 1BQF, at fixed tau=0.35 and at the efficiency-first tau](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GeorgeWilliam1999/Quantum_Track_Reconstruction/main/Toy_Characterisation/Epsilon_study_2/figures/epsilon_sensitivity/T400_workingpoints_grid.png?v=cf843162)

Figure (T = 400 working points). Solid = fixed τ = 0.35; dashed = efficiency-first τ (≥ 99% true-segment target); blue classical, red 1BQF; the dotted line marks the 99% efficiency target. The efficiency-first τ is the **largest** absolute τ whose efficiency is still ≥ 99% — keeping purity as high as the target allows — found by a grid search (robust to the discrete Hopfield levels, where a quantile lands on a degenerate band). This is the figure that answers "use the τ that gives high segment efficiency". Three readings:

1. **For the classical solver the efficiency-first τ is a small free purity gain, not a sacrifice.** It sits at **τ ≈ 0.363** — just below the true P4-end level 4/11 = 0.364 — so efficiency stays 1.000, and because 0.363 *also* clears the false 3-chain-end level (0.357) it **roughly halves the false rate** versus τ = 0.35: $`0.105\to0.056`$ at $`\sigma_{\rm res}=0.01`$, $`0.582\to0.403`$ at $`\sigma_{\rm res}=0.02`$, $`0.175\to0.095`$ along σ_scatt. (The lone exception is $`\sigma_{\rm res}=0.05`$, where the hub-indefinite solve has put true amplitudes below — even negative — so no τ reaches 99%: the search bottoms out at τ = 0.061, eff = 0.981, far = 0.99. That cell is broken at the solver level, §7.4, not a thresholding choice.)
2. **The 1BQF's 0.75 plateau is the cut, not lost physics.** At τ = 0.35 the 1-bit filter reads 0.74–0.75 efficiency across the grid (it halves the outer true band, P4-ends 4/11 → below τ). Dropping τ to the efficiency-first point (**τ ≈ 0.175**, below the false attractor 0.25) **recovers efficiency to 0.997–1.000** — proving the missing ≈ 25% was below the cut, not absent.
3. **The 1BQF price scales steeply with resolution noise.** The false rate paid to hold 99% 1BQF efficiency is **0.036 when clean** ($`\sigma_{\rm res}=0`$) but **0.658 at $`\sigma_{\rm res}=0.01`$ and 0.875 at $`\sigma_{\rm res}=0.02`$** (along σ_scatt at σ_res=0.01: 0.658 → 0.690 → 0.736). Because the efficiency-first τ ≈ 0.175 sits below the 0.25 false attractor, every noise-populated false coupling activates — the §7.4 false-coupling load, now paid in full. So at T = 400 the honest 1BQF trade is: recover full efficiency cheaply only in the low-resolution regime; above σ_res ≈ 0.01 mm, 99% efficiency costs a majority-false output.

<callout icon="✅">
	**Bottom line (T = 400).** Everything in §7 holds at production multiplicity: the classical ε-knee sits on the same zero-parameter $`(1-p)^2(1+p/2)`$ curve and the false rate follows $`c\,\varepsilon^2`$ with no refit — only the prefactor grows (≈ 45× from T = 30, the T-growth), and the formula ε keeps A sparse. On the τ the user asked for: the efficiency-first τ leaves the **classical** solver at eff = 1.000 while *halving* its false rate (τ rises to ≈ 0.363, clearing the false 3-chain ends) — a free purity gain — and **recovers the 1BQF from its 0.75 fixed-τ plateau to ≥ 99% efficiency** (τ ≈ 0.175), proving the plateau is a thresholding artefact, not lost reconstruction. The honest cost is resolution-dependent: the 1BQF pays only far = 0.036 to hold 99% efficiency when clean, but far = 0.66–0.88 once σ_res ≥ 0.01 mm, because the recovering τ drops below the 0.25 false attractor into the noise-populated false band. **Caveat:** quantum at T = 400 is store-limited to σ_res ≤ 0.02 (the σ_res = 0.05 GPU solves are absent), and at σ_res = 0.05 the classical solve is itself hub-indefinite, so neither solver has a clean 99% working point in the worst-noise corner.
</callout>
